About

Yifei Zhang is a Ph.D. in Operations Research & Industrial Engineering from The Ohio State University, working at the intersection of optimization, machine learning, reinforcement learning, and computer vision, with applications in manufacturing automation and autonomous decision-making. She previously worked as a Data Scientist at Siemens Mobility and Verizon, building and deploying end-to-end machine learning pipelines for industrial and telecom applications. She has authored several peer-reviewed publications, holds 4 patents in vision-based inspection, predictive maintenance, and industrial machine learning, and mentors graduate students and junior data scientists in algorithm development and deployment.

Projects

Automated Vision-Based Dimensional Inspection System
Honda · OSU

Designed and deployed an end-to-end computer-vision system (edge detection, ROI extraction, pretrained ResNet, regression, RL-SAC, optimal transport) to measure body-in-white gap/flush dimensions on the assembly line, with Bayesian-optimized image processing and EWMA/Hotelling control charts for statistical monitoring.

NetJets Crew Scheduling
OSU & NetJets

Developed genetic algorithms with novelty encoding and a multi-fidelity, ML-accelerated fitness evaluation to solve large-scale crew scheduling problems under hard operational constraints.

LLM-Driven Optimization Problem Formulation
OSU

Built an LLM-based framework that converts natural-language problem descriptions into formal optimization models (decision variables, objectives, constraints), including a new scoring metric for formulation accuracy, completeness, and feasibility.

Shanghai Metro Digital Intelligence O&M System
Siemens Mobility

Built a digital intelligence platform from the ground up for railway operations and maintenance, covering anomaly detection, fault diagnosis, and remaining-useful-life prediction.

Traffic Data Analysis System
Hong Kong OGCIO · Siemens Mobility

Developed traffic-speed estimation and prediction models for road segments without sensors, incorporating weather and incident effects, with an NLP pipeline for incident classification.

EEG-Based Stimulus Response Classification
OSU

Developed tree-based (random forest, XGBoost, CatBoost) and SVM classification models to predict stimulus response from brain EEG signals.
